Why Legal Codes

Law rarely exists in isolation.

A statute can tell us what the law says. Understanding what happens around that law requires a wider view: how people behave, how institutions exercise power, how technology changes ordinary life, and how social assumptions shape the way rights are understood.

Legal Codes explores those intersections through research, commentary and independent writing.

A reader's toolkit

What should you look for when researching a legal question?

  1. 01
    Start with the actual rule.

    Find the statute, regulation, judgment or official document before relying on a summary of it.

  2. 02
    Separate law from commentary.

    Know when you are reading the legal position and when you are reading someone's interpretation of it.

  3. 03
    Ask what the rule does in practice.

    A legal right on paper can interact very differently with institutions, incentives and everyday behaviour.

  4. 04
    Check the date and jurisdiction.

    Legal information is unusually sensitive to place, amendments, procedural posture and time.
